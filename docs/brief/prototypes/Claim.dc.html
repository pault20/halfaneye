“What use is half an eye? The eye is useless until every part is in place, so it cannot have been built one small step at a time.”

The claim, as commonly put in debates. Darwin raised the same worry himself in 1859, then answered it.

The short answer

Half an eye is in use today, in many forms. A ragworm larva steers with a two-cell eyespot, a flatworm with a pigment cup, a nautilus with a lensless pinhole eye, a sea star with a lensless eye on each arm. Each one works, and each is better than the stage before it.

The evidence

The simplest eyes in nature are two cells: a photoreceptor and a pigment cell that shades it from one side. In ragworm larvae that pair is wired straight to the cilia the larva swims with, enough to steer it towards light.1

1Jékely G, Colombelli J, Hausen H et al. (2008). Mechanism of phototaxis in marine zooplankton. Nature 456:395–399. doi:10.1038/nature07590

Blue sea stars have an eye of about 150 units at each arm tip, with no lenses at all. Their image is crude, but animals with intact eyes walk back to their reef when displaced; animals without them wander at random.2

2Garm A, Nilsson D-E (2014). Visual navigation in starfish. Proc R Soc B 281:20133011. doi:10.1098/rspb.2013.3011

Nilsson and Pelger calculated how many generations a flat patch of light-sensitive cells would need to become a focused camera eye, choosing every assumption to make the process slow. Their answer was fewer than 400,000 generations.3

3Nilsson D-E, Pelger S (1994). A pessimistic estimate of the time required for an eye to evolve. Proc R Soc B 256:53–58. doi:10.1098/rspb.1994.0048

Where the claim has a point

An eye is useless without a nervous system that can act on what it sees, and Nilsson and Pelger modelled only the optics. Their figure is an estimate of how fast the eye's shape could change, not a simulation of a whole animal. The living intermediates above are the stronger evidence.4

4Nilsson & Pelger 1994, as note 3. The paper describes a calculation; it is sometimes misreported as a computer simulation.
